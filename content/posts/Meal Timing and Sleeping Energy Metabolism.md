# Key Ideas

## Effect of Meal Pattern on Sleeping Energy Metabolism
### Blood Glucose levels
Both blood glucose level and energy expenditure is highest at sleep onset and declines immediately after sleep onset, and returns slightly upward 1 to 2 h before awakening.

Blood glucose levels increase with food intake and decrease with insulin secreted by the pancreas. In addition when muscle contractile activity is used, insulin is released which causes more than 80% of blood glucose to bind to insulin receptors on skeletal muscle cell membranes to be prepared for use by skeletal muscle cells.

Studies were conducted which showed that blood glucose levels increased during sleep after (1) large dinners when skipping breakfast or (2) later than usual dinners, but had no effect on total energy consumption.

Skipping breakfast has no connection to energy consumption according to this study

Sleeping 4 h for one week increases insulin resistance and results in hyperglycemia similar to that seen in the early stages of diabetes.

## The Relationship between Energy Metabolism and Sleep
Ghrelin and orexin promote eating and arousal, Leptin and insulin promote satiety, energy metabolism, and sleep. Blood leptin levels are lower in and ghrelin was was higher in short sleep duration, this lead to increased hunger and appetite especially for high calorie foods with high carbs in some studies.

Carb intakes activates brain areas in reward and motor activity which leads to short term cognitive improvements and eventual sleepiness and sleep onset. Significant reduction in mean sleep onset latency is observed with high carb meals 4 h before bedtime. Mechanism here might be the direct stimulation of ventrolateral preoptic nucleus in the hypothalamus by glucose, which plays a crucial role in the induction and maintenance of slow-wave-sleep. The effects of elevated systemic glucose levels on sleep may be due to effects of insulin on the central nervous system. When infused into the cerebral ventricular system, insulin promotes SWS. Furthermore, elevated glucose and subsequent insulin secretion increase the delivery of tryptophan and its conversion to serotonin in the brain, promoting sleep. Also insulin promotes the selective uptake of large neutral amino acids by muscles, these LNAA's interfere with tryptophan delivery to the brain so this could also be why insulin causes sleepiness.

## Effects of Time-restricted eating on sleep, and its mechanisms, benefits and translational potential

Time restricted eating where breakfast was delayed to 2h after waking up and dinner was pushed to 4 h before bedtime was shown in some studies to decrease daily caloric intake, bodyweight, increase cardiometabolic health without adverse effects on sleep.

It also increases insulin sensitivity.

## Implications

Breakfast time is 56% inheritable and Dinner time is 0% inheritable.

Skipping breakfast also can lead to poor quality of life, poor dier, and lack of physical activity.

There are food components which directly affect sleep such as tryptophan in milk which is metabolized to melatonin which is metabolized to serotonin. Caffeine binds to adenosine receptors that promote drowsiness, keeping us awake.
